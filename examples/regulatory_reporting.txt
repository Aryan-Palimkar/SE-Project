A building society has been told by its prudential regulator that its quarterly capital and liquidity returns must be submitted in a new XBRL-based format from 1 January next year. The data items, validation rules and submission deadlines are set out in the regulator's published reporting framework, and they will not change before go-live.

The society has awarded a fixed-price contract to a supplier to build a reporting system that extracts balances from the core banking ledger and the treasury system every quarter, maps them to the regulator's templates, runs the published validation rules and produces a submission file. The core banking ledger is a twenty-five-year-old mainframe, and its extract formats are documented but cannot be changed.

Every figure in a return must be traceable back to the source ledger entries, and the internal audit team will sign off each release after reviewing a full test report against the regulator's validation rules. A wrong or late return can lead to a fine and supervisory action. The finance team will be available for acceptance testing in the final two months of the project. The work uses the supplier's established reporting product, and the team of about twelve people has built similar returns for other lenders.
