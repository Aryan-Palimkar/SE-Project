A consumer lending fintech wants to replace the spreadsheet-driven credit decisions behind its personal loans with an automated decision service. Applicants apply through the website and mobile app; the service pulls their credit bureau report and bank-account transaction data through open-banking APIs, runs affordability and fraud checks, and returns approve, decline or refer within a few seconds. Referred applications go to an underwriter's queue.

The credit risk team changes the lending rules constantly: cut-off scores, affordability buffers and fraud rules are adjusted every few weeks as default rates and competitor offers move, and the product manager wants new rules in production within days of being agreed. The platform team already deploys the website several times a week through an automated pipeline on a public cloud, and the decision service is expected to ship the same way.

The service handles bank statements, income details and national identity numbers, and last year the company fought off a credential-stuffing attack on its customer login. The consumer credit regulator expects every decision to be explainable to the applicant, and declined applicants can ask why. The product manager sits with the team every day, and the first version should be live within four months.
