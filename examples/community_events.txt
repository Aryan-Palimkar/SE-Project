A three-person startup is building a mobile app that helps neighbours organise small local events: street clean-ups, book swaps, pick-up football. Users create an event, invite people nearby and chat in the event's group. The founders are not sure which features people actually want, so the plan is to put a basic version in front of a few hundred users in one city and change it based on what they do.

The founders talk to users every week and expect the feature list to change after almost every round of feedback. They want to ship updates to the app stores every week. The app stores only a display name, an email address and an approximate location for each user; there are no payments. The backend is a standard managed database and serverless functions, which the team has used before. If the app goes down, events are simply organised in other ways for a day. One of the founders acts as product owner and sits with the developer every day, and there is no fixed budget or scope: the seed funding covers about nine months.
